#1"headless MCP" surfaced as top enterprise blocker

Turning four discovery calls into an agentic AI roadmap

When Beefree's MCP server hit beta, feedback was scattered across sales calls, support tickets and Slack threads. I built a repeatable framework to find the signal underneath the noise.

Context

Beefree's MCP server marked a strategic shift — from a standalone content editor to a foundational "agent-based" ecosystem. Partners weren't asking for an editor anymore; they were building multi-agent systems where Beefree needed to act as the "hand" for an external "brain." The problem: nobody had synthesized what that actually required.

Approach

I ran structured discovery calls with four enterprise partners building production multi-agent systems, then applied a Jobs-to-be-Done forces framework — push, pull, anxiety, habit — to separate one-off feature requests from the underlying jobs customers were actually hiring the MCP to do. That surfaced three core jobs: High-Volume Campaign Generation, Brand-Compliant Personalization, and Developer Experience.

What the evidence said

"Headless MCP is something that we would value a lot... many use cases require producing content offline."— Implementation lead, enterprise partner
"We need to severely restrict the creativity of this agent. It's not allowed to change the structure."— Compliance lead, regulated-industry partner
"I'm not a coder... I've pretty much got an enterprise-level production-ready software that I've vibe coded."— Non-technical founder, SMB partner

Outcome

I synthesized the findings into six validated opportunity hypotheses, ready for roadmap review:

Headless CapabilityUnlock high-volume production without an open editor session
Deterministic GuardrailsAPI-level locking so agents can't touch legal/brand elements
AI-Ready DocumentationDocs structured for LLM ingestion, not just humans
Native Tool GroupingSelective tool exposure for large agentic systems
Server-Side Template AnalysisBatch ingestion for legacy template libraries
Model/Provider ConfigurabilityFirst-class support for EU hosting & model choice

The biggest reframe: "headless architecture" moved from a backlog nice-to-have to the single highest-priority blocker to enterprise adoption — now driving the next roadmap cycle.

Opening a new market with a $500K Google partnership

Middle schools couldn't afford a physics lab. I shipped one — custom hardware, firmware, and a Google-integrated app — that reached 1M+ downloads.

Context

Arduino Science Journal was already gaining traction in high schools and universities as an affordable alternative to expensive lab equipment. Researching how educators were using it in the classroom — including a workshop led by a physics professor at La Sapienza University in Rome — pointed to an underserved opportunity: middle school, a segment Arduino didn't serve yet.

Challenge

Opening a new segment meant real constraints, not hypothetical ones: mapping content to the NGSS and UK National Science Curriculum, meeting health & safety compliance for physical kits, and building for COPPA/GDPR compliance since the end users were minors.

Approach

I ran a Kanban/Waterfall process for hardware development — where lead times and physical constraints demand a different cadence — alongside an Agile process for content, design and Android software, so firmware could keep iterating on what we learned from content testing. I led the partnership negotiation with Google to integrate a custom Arduino MKR WiFi 1010 board over BLE directly into the official Google Science Journal app, coordinating engineering teams across both companies against an announced launch deadline.

Outcome

Shipped a fully custom hardware kit, firmware, and CMS (content available in 6 languages) integrated into Google's app on the Play Store — showcased live at NSTA19 in St. Louis alongside Google's booth. The partnership also secured $500K to fund the long-term product roadmap, and the new middle-school segment grew total users by 30%.

Shipping AI features on a no-code B2B SaaS platform

Context

Beefree is a no-code B2B SaaS platform used to build email, landing page and pop-up content. As AI capability moved from novelty to expectation, the challenge was building AI-native workflows without breaking the simplicity that makes a no-code tool work in the first place.
